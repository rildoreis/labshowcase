3D Printers

Creality Ender-3 S1 — two FDM printers for prototypes, props and tactile resources

What is it?

Desktop 3D printers that build objects layer by layer from plastic filament. Students and researchers design a model (or download one), prepare it in a slicer and print it in minutes to hours. In an education lab the printers turn ideas into tangible things: tactile maps and letters, game pieces, replicas of cultural objects, phone stands, spare parts for other equipment.

What's in the kit

  • 2 Creality Ender-3 S1 printers with direct-drive extruder and automatic bed levelling
  • Print area 220 × 220 × 270 mm; PLA, PETG and TPU filament
  • Software: Creality Print, Ultimaker Cura or PrusaSlicer; models from Thingiverse or Printables

Used so far

Nobody in the lab has tried this one yet. If you know it, come and show us — a 20-minute walkthrough would be the first entry here.

Ideas for teaching and research

Teaching ideas

  • Tactile materials: letters, syllables, maps and symbols in relief for literacy and accessibility.
  • Game pieces and boards: custom tokens and tiles for vocabulary and grammar games.
  • Cultural replicas: objects, monuments and artefacts to accompany language and culture units.
  • Design thinking: the full cycle of model, print, test and improve.

Research ideas

  • Accessible-resource studies: tactile materials for learners with visual impairment.
  • Maker pedagogy: what changes when students fabricate their own learning objects.
  • Lab infrastructure: printing mounts, stands and parts for research setups.

Good to know

  • Not yet investigated in detail — the first step is a test print and a short written procedure.
  • The nozzle and bed get hot; supervise students and let prints cool before removing them.
  • Print with PLA in a ventilated room; store filament dry.
